How NutriFit works

What the app calculates, how the models were tested, what was wrong with the data, and where the results should not be trusted.

From profile to meal plan

  1. Estimate energy needs

    The Mifflin-St Jeor equation gives resting calories from age, sex, height and weight. An activity factor scales that to daily use, then a goal adjustment applies: about 20% less for weight loss, 10% more for muscle gain. Calories never drop below 1,200 (women) or 1,500 (men).

  2. Set macro targets

    Protein is 1.4, 1.8 or 2.0 g per kg of body weight depending on goal, capped at 35% of calories. Fat is about 27% of calories and carbohydrate takes the rest.

  3. Profile the foods with K-Means

    Each food is described by its calories, protein, carbohydrate, fat and fibre per 100 g. K-Means groups the {{ metrics.data_quality.rows_in_meal_pool }} usable foods into {{ metrics.kmeans.chosen_k }} nutrient profiles. The number of groups was chosen by comparing silhouette scores, not picked by hand.

  4. Search for the best-fitting day

    For each meal the planner tries thousands of combinations of foods and portion sizes and keeps the one closest to that meal's share of your targets, with a small preference for foods that are high in protein and fibre and low in saturated fat. “Show another plan” samples a different set of candidates.

Cleaning the data

The food table is a flattened copy of a printed handbook, so it needed repair before it could be used.

  • Numbers such as 1,373 kept their thousands separator and were silently read as zero. They are now parsed correctly.
  • Trace markers (t), ranges (8-44) and unavailable-value sentinels (-1) are handled explicitly instead of being replaced with zero.
  • {{ metrics.data_quality.rows_dropped_missing }} rows with missing core values and {{ metrics.data_quality.rows_dropped_inconsistent }} rows whose calories disagreed with their macros (4×protein + 4×carbs + 9×fat) were dropped.
  • Many rows were fragments of a parent heading (“Lima”, “Raw, grated”). Foods used in meals were renamed by hand, and unusable ones, like raw rice and cream, were removed.

Limits

  • Mifflin-St Jeor is an estimate and is typically within about 10% of measured resting energy use for most adults.
  • The food data is old and small. A larger source such as USDA FoodData Central would improve names, serving sizes and variety.
  • Allergies, vegetarian or vegan diets and cultural food preferences are not modelled yet.
  • NutriFit is an educational project, not medical advice.

Built with

Python, Flask, pandas, NumPy and scikit-learn on the back end, with server-rendered templates and hand-written CSS. The nutrition formulas, data cleaning and planner have automated tests.
